# Zuri Nilotica Design Direction

## Goal

The website should build trust before it sells. Customers need to feel that Zuri Nilotica is authentic, Ugandan, traceable, and worth buying because the product has a real source and a real process behind it.

## Visual Direction

The site should stay colorful and confident, inspired by the playful energy of Bansk, but grounded in Uganda, shea butter, and natural skincare.

Recommended palette direction:

- Bansk sky blue: `#BEE1F7`
- Soft yellow: `#f1eb9c`
- Soft pink: `#fec3d7`
- Pop pink: `#df1995`
- Burgundy: `#6D2D33`
- Warm cream: `#f9f4ee`
- Fresh green accent: `#67a671`

Avoid making the site too beige, too dark green, or too serious. It should feel natural, bright, editorial, and purchase-worthy.

Use solid color blocks more than gradients. The visual mood should feel close to a bright Coolors-style palette: clean, punchy, easy to remember, and playful enough to echo the Bansk inspiration.

## Homepage Flow

The homepage should not push "Buy Now" too early. It should create desire first:

1. Hero: introduce purity and origin.
2. Origin: make Uganda and Nilotica shea feel special.
3. Collection cards: introduce products and invite exploration.
4. Trust sections: explain why the product is rare, pure, impactful, and versatile.
5. Story cards: teach customers about the tree, process, and communities.
6. Footer: act as a clean brand signature.

## CTA Strategy

Homepage CTAs should invite exploration:

- "Meet the Collection"
- "Explore This Product"
- "Read the Story"
- "See the Process"
- "Meet the Makers"

Product detail pages should sell:

- "Buy Now"
- "Order Now"
- "Add to Bag"

This keeps the homepage from feeling pushy while still moving customers toward purchase.

## Story Pages To Build Later

The three story cards should eventually link to dedicated pages:

1. The Nilotica Shea Tree
   - Explain what makes Vitellaria nilotica different.
   - Focus on Uganda's Nile Basin, rarity, texture, and benefits.

2. Hand-Harvested, Sun-Dried, Cold-Pressed
   - Show the process step by step.
   - Use imagery and simple copy to make the product feel clean and trustworthy.

3. Partnership, Not Extraction
   - Explain community impact, fair sourcing, and respect for harvesters.
   - Make customers feel that buying supports a better sourcing model.

Story page layout:

- Big page title and short persuasive intro.
- One visual collage at the top.
- Content below the collage in clear editorial panels.
- Final call-to-action leading back to the collection.

Avoid alternating image/text/image/text pages for these stories. They feel too basic and break the reading rhythm.

## Footer Direction

The footer should stay simple:

- Brand wordmark.
- One short origin sentence.
- Main navigation links.
- Tagline, copyright, privacy, and location.

Article cards do not belong in the footer. They need space in their own section so they feel intentional and readable.
